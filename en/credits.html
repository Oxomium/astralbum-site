Credits and data sources

English translation provided for information; the French version prevails.

Astralbum presents real celestial objects, with data published by the scientific community. The organizations and authors cited below have no connection with Astralbum and do not endorse it. Values marked “≈” are estimated or calculated by the game from this data.

Stars

  • Gaia DR3 (ESA): this game uses data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia, processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC). Funding for the DPAC is provided by national institutions, in particular those participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement. Credit: ESA, Gaia DPAC. References: Gaia Collaboration, Prusti et al. 2016, A&A 595, A1 (the Gaia mission); Gaia Collaboration, Vallenari et al. 2023, A&A 674, A1 (Gaia DR3). Data license: CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/3.0/igo/); commercial use authorized by ESA on [date] (ref. [reference]).
  • Acknowledgement requested by ESA: “This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/gaia), processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC, https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dpac/consortium). Funding for the DPAC has been provided by national institutions, in particular the institutions participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement.”
  • Hipparcos (ESA): credit: ESA; commercial use authorized by ESA on [date] (ref. [reference]). The Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues (ESA 1997, ESA SP-1200) and the new reduction by F. van Leeuwen (2007, A&A 474, 653), for the stars that Gaia does not measure reliably (mostly the brightest ones), as well as for HIP and HD numbers and spectral types.
  • Star names: IAU Catalog of Star Names, International Astronomical Union Working Group on Star Names (IAU WGSN, iau.org), IAU content under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The IAU does not sell star names and does not recognize any purchased name; Astralbum never allows a celestial object to be named.
  • Common designations of some stars (Bayer and Flamsteed designations, Gliese and Jahreiss catalog numbers, common names such as “Kapteyn's Star”): HYG database, version 4.1, by David Nash (astronexus, github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database), under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Only the designations were used, then translated into French; the resulting list is shared under the same license.
  • Positions of a few objects (stars, active galactic nuclei): SIMBAD database, CDS, Strasbourg. “This research has made use of the SIMBAD database, operated at CDS, Strasbourg, France.”

Exoplanets

  • The Extrasolar Planets Encyclopaedia (exoplanet.eu), Observatoire de Paris – PSL, CNRS (Schneider et al. 2011, A&A 532, A79): planet data, and the temperature, radius and mass of their stars (their distance comes from Gaia when Gaia measures it). Content under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); data selected and converted for the game. “This research has made use of data obtained from or tools provided by the portal exoplanet.eu of The Extrasolar Planets Encyclopaedia.”
  • NASA Exoplanet Archive (Caltech/IPAC, NASA): data for confirmed planets missing from exoplanet.eu and verification of the card values. “This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program.”
  • Exoplanet names: International Astronomical Union (IAU), NameExoWorlds campaigns 2015, 2019 and 2022 (nameexoworlds.iau.org; IAU content under the CC BY 4.0 license). The IAU has no connection with Astralbum and does not endorse it.
  • Habitability: Bohl A., Lawrence L., Lowry G., Kaltenegger L., “Probing the limits of habitability: a catalogue of rocky exoplanets in the habitable zone”, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 547, stag028 (2026), doi:10.1093/mnras/stag028; data: Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.18134528; article and data under the CC BY 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Habitable zone: Kopparapu et al. 2013.
  • Planets published after this catalog, under the CC BY 4.0 license: Jiang C. et al., “Multicolour validation of two temperate mini-Neptunes around M-dwarf habitable zones”, MNRAS 547, stag367 (2026), doi:10.1093/mnras/stag367 (TOI-2094 b); Peláez-Torres A. et al., “A gem system with a lava world and a habitable zone sub-Neptune orbiting TOI-1752”, MNRAS 548 (2026), doi:10.1093/mnras/stag713 (TOI-1752 c); Pagés Navarrete J. F., “Characterization of the Compact Multiplanet System TOI-789”, The Astronomical Journal 172, 251 (2026), doi:10.3847/1538-3881/aea6c9 (TOI-789 d).

Pulsars and magnetars

  • ATNF Pulsar Catalogue (https://www.atnf.csiro.au/research/pulsar/psrcat; Manchester R. N., Hobbs G. B., Teoh A. & Hobbs M. 2005, AJ 129, 1993), © CSIRO, under the 3-Clause BSD License (text below).

Asteroids and comets

  • JPL Small-Body Database (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov), NASA/JPL-Caltech.

Solar System (Cosmic objects)

  • Card data: NASA Planetary Fact Sheet (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, NSSDCA), source: NASA.
  • Mars mini-game: views of the planet computed from the global map of Mars from NASA 3D Resources (map: NASA/JPL-Caltech, from Viking images processed by the USGS). NASA and JPL have no connection with Astralbum and do not endorse it.
  • Cosmic mini-games: NASA values (rotation and orbital periods, radii of Saturn's rings, moons of Jupiter); sunspots after Snodgrass and Ulrich (1990, ApJ 351, 309).
  • Earth in the loading animation: continent outlines from Natural Earth (“ne_110m_land”, naturalearthdata.com), public domain. Made with Natural Earth.

Black holes

  • Mergers: “This research has made use of data or software obtained from the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center (gwosc.org)”, a service of the LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA collaborations. Data under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), selected and converted for the game. References: R. Abbott et al. 2023, ApJS 267, 29; R. Abbott et al. 2021, SoftwareX 13, 100658.
  • Supermassive black holes: compilation by R. C. E. van den Bosch (2016, ApJ 831, 134; github.com/remcovandenbosch/Black-Hole-Mass-compilation), under the MIT License (text below).

Constellations

  • Official IAU constellation boundaries (Delporte, 1930), following the method and table of N. Roman (1987, PASP 99, 695; catalog VI/42 of the VizieR service of CDS, Strasbourg), redistributed by the Astropy project under the 3-Clause BSD License (text below).

Glossary

The glossary definitions were written for the game from the following scientific sources:

  • International Astronomical Union (IAU): 2006 Resolution B5 (definition of planets and dwarf planets), 2012 Resolution B2 (astronomical unit), 2015 Resolution B3 (nominal values for the Sun and the planets), the 88 constellations.
  • International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM): International System of Units (kelvin, tesla).
  • NASA: NASA Science (Sun, stars, neutron stars, pulsars and magnetars, black holes, asteroids, comets), NASA Exoplanet Archive, Center for Near Earth Object Studies (near-Earth objects and potentially hazardous asteroids), DART mission.
  • European Space Agency (ESA): Gaia and Rosetta missions, comet families.
  • Pulsars and magnetars: ATNF Pulsar Catalogue (Manchester et al. 2005, AJ 129, 1993); McGill Online Magnetar Catalog (Olausen and Kaspi 2014, ApJS 212, 6).
  • Gravitational waves: LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA collaborations; Abbott et al. 2016, Physical Review Letters 116, 061102 (first detection); Abbott et al. 2020, Physical Review Letters 125, 101102 (GW190521).
  • Supermassive black holes: Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration 2019, ApJL 875, L1 (M87*) and 2022, ApJL 930, L12 (Sagittarius A*). Nobel Foundation: Physics Prizes 1993, 2017, 2019 and 2020.
  • Exoplanets: Borucki et al. 2011, ApJ 736, 19; Fressin et al. 2013, ApJ 766, 81; Fulton et al. 2017, AJ 154, 109; Kopparapu et al. 2013, ApJ 765, 131; Bohl et al. 2026, MNRAS 547, stag028.
  • Exoplanet atmospheres: Charbonneau et al. 2002, ApJ 568, 377 (sodium); Vidal-Madjar et al. 2003, Nature 422, 143 (hydrogen); Spake et al. 2018, Nature 557, 68 (helium); Ehrenreich et al. 2020, Nature 580, 597 (iron); JWST Transiting Exoplanet Community ERS Team 2023, Nature 614, 649 (carbon dioxide); Tsai et al. 2023, Nature 617, 483 (sulfur dioxide); Fu et al. 2024, Nature 632, 752 (hydrogen sulfide); Fortney et al. 2008, ApJ 678, 1419 (titanium and vanadium oxides).
  • Asteroids and comets: Tholen 1984 (thesis, University of Arizona); DeMeo et al. 2009, Icarus 202, 160; Levison 1996, ASP Conference Series 107, 173.

Constants and formulas

  • Constants: IAU nominal values (2015 Resolution B3) for the radius and luminosity of the Sun; mass of the Sun derived from the gravitational parameter GM☉ of the same resolution.
  • Color and temperature: Ballesteros (2012); bolometric corrections: Andrae et al. (2018), Flower (1996) as corrected by Torres (2010).
  • Equilibrium temperature of exoplanets, radius and gravity at the horizon of black holes (r = 2GM/c², g = c⁴/4GM), average orbital speed of asteroids: standard physics formulas.
